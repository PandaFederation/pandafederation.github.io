# Free people-search and data-broker removal guide

**Verified November 10, 2026.** Published by Panda Federation at https://pandafederation.com/data-broker-removal/

_Not legal advice. This page is a research guide built from official sources and the sites' own pages, checked on the date shown. It does not apply any law to your situation. For advice, talk to a licensed attorney in your state._

## Order of steps

1. **Inventory first.** List the emails, phone numbers and old accounts you still control. Search each inbox for sign-up and marketing mail to find forgotten accounts.
2. **Delete old accounts while you can still sign in.** Some sites only accept deletion from a signed-in session. Finish those before you close the email address that gets their codes.
3. **Find your listings and confirm they are you.** Common names pull up other people. Only opt out of a record that is actually yours.
4. **Opt out at the official link, in your own browser.** Use the links below. Captchas often fail in automated or remote browsers, so open them yourself.
5. **Click verification emails right away.** Links commonly expire in 24 hours, and some much sooner. One in our testing expired in about 15 minutes.
6. **Save the receipt.** Keep the confirmation email or screenshot. Log the result from that message, not from memory. A reply that says denied is easy to misread as done.
7. **Recheck later.** Look again after the window the site gives (often 3 days), then every month or two. Listings can come back when new public records arrive.

## Pitfalls

- Never pay a people-search site to remove you. Their opt-outs are free.
- Delete linked accounts before you delete an old email address.
- Where you live matters. Many sites only grant formal privacy rights to residents of states with a comprehensive privacy law. Everyone else gets a courtesy opt-out, if that.
- Most state laws exclude publicly available information, such as government records. That is what many listings are built from, so a legal deletion right may not force a takedown.
- Do not misstate your state of residence on a form.
- If an account-deletion email tells you not to sign back in, don't. Signing in can cancel the deletion on some sites.

### Rights depend on your state

SmartBackgroundChecks, USPhonebook and FastPeopleSearch publish near-identical privacy notices. They say your privacy rights depend on your state of residence and point residents of states with comprehensive privacy laws to a supplemental notice. They take requests through a web form or by phone. They also say their data is publicly available information, which they consider outside state privacy laws, and that they apply opt-outs to it as a courtesy. If you live in a state without a comprehensive law, such as Arizona, expect a formal privacy request to be refused. The opt-out form is still worth trying.
