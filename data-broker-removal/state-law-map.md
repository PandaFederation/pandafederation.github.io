# US state data-deletion rights map

**Verified November 10, 2026.** Published by Panda Federation at https://pandafederation.com/data-broker-removal/#state-map

_Not legal advice. This page is a research guide built from official sources and the sites' own pages, checked on the date shown. It does not apply any law to your situation. For advice, talk to a licensed attorney in your state._

## Summary

- 19 states have a comprehensive privacy law in effect with a right to delete: CA, CO, CT, DE, IN, IA, KY, MD, MN, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OR, RI, TN, TX, UT, VA.
- Florida has a deletion right too, but it only covers companies with more than $1 billion in yearly revenue that also meet another test. Most people-search sites will not qualify.
- Enacted, not yet in effect: Oklahoma and Louisiana (January 1, 2027), Alabama (May 1, 2027), Vermont (January 1, 2028). Vermont already requires data brokers to register.
- Data-broker registries: California, Texas, Oregon and Vermont. Only California has a one-request deletion tool (DROP), and it is for California residents.
- Arkansas: we found no enacted comprehensive privacy law. Some sites cite Act 549 of 2023, but the official Act 549 is a firearms bill.

## Why a right to delete may not remove a listing

Most of these laws exclude publicly available information (government records and similar) from the personal data they cover. People-search listings are largely built from those records. So even where a deletion right is in effect, a site may treat its listing as outside the law and offer only a courtesy opt-out. California's Delete Act and DROP are the strongest broker-specific tool we verified.

## Arizona

Arizona has no comprehensive consumer privacy law, so Arizona residents have no general legal right to make a people-search site delete a listing. The Arizona Attorney General's consumer pages cover data-breach notice, not deletion, and SB 1815 (2026) was only introduced. Arizona residents can still use each site's free opt-out form. Many sites honor those as a courtesy. Sites that tie formal rights to state law may refuse.
